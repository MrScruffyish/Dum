# Art and sound in Dum

All pictures and sound effects are generated by code when the game starts
(`src/client/textures.cpp` and `src/client/sound.cpp`). You can replace any of them with
your own PNG or WAV file without touching or recompiling the code.

## Quick start

```sh
./build/dum --export-art        # writes every picture to data/gfx/*.png
```

1. Open a file in `data/gfx/`, for example `monster_goblin.png`, in GIMP, Aseprite, Krita
   or any other paint program.
2. Paint over it, or replace it with a new picture, and save it as PNG with the same name.
3. Start the game. Your file is used instead of the generated picture.
4. Delete the file to get the generated picture back.

Only the files you keep in `data/gfx/` override anything. A good habit is to export, then
**delete the files you don't intend to change**. Otherwise a later improvement to the built-in
art won't show up, because your exported copy wins.

## Picture names and sizes

The sizes below are those of the generated art. You can use other sizes as long as you
follow the rules in the last column.

| Name | Generated size | What it is | Rules |
|------|---------------|------------|-------|
| `wall_brick`, `wall_cave`, `wall_moss`, `wall_dark`, `wall_wood`, `wall_blood` | 64x64 | wall textures (the dungeon picks a theme per room/depth) | power of two (32, 64, 128, 256...), tiles seamlessly |
| `floor_stone`, `floor_dirt`, `floor_tiles`, `floor_wood` | 64x64 | floor textures | power of two, seamless |
| `ceiling`, `ceiling_cave` | 64x64 | ceilings (cave ceiling above dirt floors) | power of two, seamless |
| `door`, `door_locked` | 64x64 | closed doors (fills a whole wall tile) | power of two |
| `rubble` | 64x64 | rubble blocking a corridor | power of two |
| `stairs_down`, `stairs_up`, `stairs_up_ceiling` | 64x64 | floor of stair tiles, light shaft above up-stairs | power of two |
| `monster_<id>` | 64x64 | monster, standing pose | any size, transparent background |
| `monster_<id>_attack` | 64x64 | monster, attack pose (shown while it attacks/casts) | same size as the standing pose |
| `player_<class>` / `player_<class>_attack` | 64x64 | how other players see your class in co-op | any size, transparent |
| `item_<id>` | 32x32 | items lying on the floor | any size, transparent |
| `hand_fist`, `hand_dagger`, `hand_sword`, `hand_mace`, `hand_axe`, `hand_staff`, `hand_bow` | 128x128 | the first-person weapon in your hand | any size; drawn at the bottom right of the view |
| `spell_<id>` | 32x32 | glowing projectile / explosion of a spell | black = invisible (it is *added* to the screen like light) |
| `arrow` | 32x32 | arrows and sling stones in flight | transparent |

`<id>` is the id from the data files: `[monster goblin]` → `monster_goblin.png`,
`[item potion_heal]` → `item_potion_heal.png`, `[class wizard]` → `player_wizard.png`.
New monsters and items you add to the data files get generated art automatically, and you
can override it the same way.

### How sprites are drawn (important when you make your own)

- **The bottom of the picture stands on the floor.** The whole picture is scaled to the
  creature's height in the world: `0.85 × size` of a wall height, where `size` comes from
  `monsters.txt`. Small creatures like rats are drawn in the *lower part* of the 64x64 image
  with empty space above. If you fill the whole image with a rat, you get a big rat. Either
  leave space, or adjust `size` in `monsters.txt`.
- **Width follows the image's aspect ratio**, so a 64x32 picture is half as wide as tall.
- **Transparency:** pixels with alpha 0 are see-through. Every other pixel is drawn fully
  opaque. There is no partial transparency (keeps the crisp retro look).
- **Bats and wraiths** (sprite `bat` / `ghost`) float above the floor. Wraiths are drawn
  with a checkerboard see-through effect automatically.
- **Light:** paint everything **as if in bright daylight**. The game darkens pictures by
  distance and torch light, and flashes monsters red when hit. Spell pictures are the
  exception: they glow and are never darkened.
- **Bosses** get a crown added to the generated picture. Your own picture is used as-is.
- Keep a dark 1-pixel outline around sprites. It reads much better in dark corridors.

### Style tips

- The game renders at 640x360 and scales up with sharp pixels, so detail finer than
  the 64x64 grid is mostly wasted on monsters further than a few steps away.
  128x128 sprites are fine for bosses that you'll see up close.
- Use a slightly limited palette per area (brown/grey stone near the top, darker and
  redder deeper down) to keep the levels coherent.
- Wall textures must tile: the left edge continues the right edge, and top continues bottom.

## Sound effects

Put WAV files in `data/sfx/` (create the folder). Any sample rate, 8/16-bit or float,
mono or stereo. They are converted to mono at load time (the game pans them itself).

| File | Plays when |
|------|-----------|
| `swing.wav` | you swing a melee weapon |
| `hit.wav` / `crit.wav` | something is hit / critically hit |
| `miss.wav` | an attack misses |
| `shoot.wav` | a bow or sling is fired |
| `cast.wav` | a spell is cast (you or a monster) |
| `explode.wav` | fireball / holy burst / explosions |
| `hurt.wav` | you take damage |
| `door.wav` | a door opens or closes, a lock is picked |
| `pickup.wav` | you pick something up |
| `die.wav` | a monster or player dies |
| `levelup.wav` | you gain a level |
| `growl.wav` | a monster wakes up nearby (your early warning!) |
| `step.wav` | your footsteps |
| `stairs.wav` | the party takes the stairs |
| `heal.wav` | you are healed |

Sounds from other places in the dungeon get quieter with distance (silent beyond 20 tiles)
and are panned left/right by direction. Keep effects short (under a second) and trimmed at
the start, or they will feel late. Normalize them to about -3 dB. The game mixes up to
24 sounds at once and soft-limits the output.

There is no music yet. It would go in the same folder later.

## Changing the generated art in code

To change the built-in look instead of using PNGs, edit `src/client/textures.cpp`:
`brickWall()`, `caveWall()` and similar functions make the textures, `humanoid()`,
`caster()`, `rat()` and similar functions draw the monster types, `itemSprite()` draws the
items and `viewModel()` draws the weapons in your hand. A monster's `sprite`, `color` and
`color2` in `monsters.txt` pick the drawing function and the body and eye colors, so you
can get a new-looking monster with no code at all.
